# SWE to HCI PhD: Strategic Learning & Content Roadmap

## 1. Context & Motivation
As a Software Engineer (SWE) transitioning into Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) research:
- **Your Edge**: Technical prototyping ability, systems understanding, software tooling skills. You can build things researchers without engineering backgrounds struggle to prototype.
- **The Gap to Close**: Formulating **HCI research questions** (not just "can we build it?", but "what human capability does this unlock?", "how does interaction change cognitive models?", "what are the trade-offs in user agency?"), and mastering **empirical research methodologies** (qualitative user studies, quantitative evaluation, thematic coding).
- **The Social Media Strategy**: "Learning in public" turns your self-study into a portfolio. Faculty advisors look for intellectual curiosity, writing clarity, and domain awareness.

### Pillar 1: Theoretical Foundations
- **Direct Manipulation & Mental Models**: Don Norman (*The Design of Everyday Things*), Ben Shneiderman.
- **Cognitive Dimensions & Distributed Cognition**: Edwin Hutchins, Mark Weiser (*The Computer for the 21st Century* - Ubiquitous Computing).
- **Human-AI Interaction**: Amershi et al. (*Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction*, CHI 2019), mixed-initiative systems (Horvitz).

### Pillar 2: Empirical Evaluation & Methodology
- Understanding the difference between an engineering milestone and an HCI contribution:
  - *Engineering*: Does it run fast? Does it scale?
  - *HCI*: How do users experience it? Does it augment human agency or reduce cognitive friction? What are the behavioral patterns?
- Methods: Semi-structured interviews, think-aloud protocols, Likert scales (NASA-TLX, SUS), within/between-subjects experiments.

### Pillar 3: Contemporary Frontiers (High-Relevance Tracks for SWEs)
- **Interactive AI / Human-Centered AI (HCAI)**: Generative UI, steering LLMs, human-in-the-loop debugging, prompt interfaces.
- **Developer Experience & Program Synthesis Tools**: Making programming, design, or data exploration faster and more intuitive.
- **UIST-style Systems & Toolkits**: Creating new frameworks or runtimes for novel interaction paradigms.

### Pillar 4: Learning in Public & Portfolio Building
- Weekly digest / newsletter breaking down 1 foundational paper + 1 hot recent paper (from CHI / UIST / CSCW).
- Visual social media notes (takeaways, system architecture vs user interaction, critiques).
- Mini-replications: take an interesting paper and build a 48-hour prototype demo.

## 3. Recommended Phased Roadmap (6–12 Months)

| Phase | Duration | Focus | Public Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1: Foundations & Vocabulary** | Month 1–2 | Classic papers, core terminology, top HCI conferences breakdown | 2 posts/week: "Core HCI Concepts Explained for Engineers" |
| **Phase 2: Modern Papers & HCAI** | Month 3–5 | CHI / UIST 2024–2026 papers on GenAI interaction, agentic interfaces | Weekly newsletter: "The Interactive AI / Systems HCI Digest" |
| **Phase 3: Methods & Critique** | Month 6–8 | User study design, qualitative coding, reading paper critiques | In-depth breakdowns: "What makes this user study convincing or flawed?" |
| **Phase 4: Research Ideation & Outreach** | Month 9–12 | Formulating research proposals, reaching out to potential advisors | 1 open-source prototype + accompanying research write-up |

## 4. Top Venues to Track
- **ACM CHI** (Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems) — the flagship general venue.
- **ACM UIST** (User Interface Software and Technology) — ideal for SWEs (systems, tools, interaction techniques).
- **ACM CSCW** (Computer-Supported Cooperative Work and Social Computing) — collaborative tools, social dynamics.
- **ACM DIS** (Designing Interactive Systems) — design theory, artifacts.
- **ACM IUI** (Intelligent User Interfaces) — AI + interaction.
