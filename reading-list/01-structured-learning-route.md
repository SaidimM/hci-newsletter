# 12-Week Structured "Learn by Doing" HCI Route

> **Target**: Software Engineer transitioning into Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) research & PhD applications.  
> **Philosophy**: No passive reading without immediate field work. Every sprint produces an empirical artifact, prototype, or public note.

## Sprint 1 (Weeks 1–2): Heuristic Evaluation & Mental Models

### 1. Key Readings
- Don Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (Chapters 1 & 2: Affordances, Signifiers, Conceptual Models, Gulf of Execution & Evaluation).
- Jakob Nielsen, *10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design* (NN/g).
- Paper: *Heuristic Evaluation of User Interfaces* (Nielsen & Molich, CHI 1990).

### 2. The Field Work (Your Practical Task)
- Pick a complex technical tool (e.g., Docker Desktop, GitKraken, AWS Console, Postman, or Cursor).
- Walk through 2 core user tasks (e.g., "setting up a multi-container network" or "resolving a merge conflict").
- Identify **5 concrete heuristic violations**.
- Rate each on Nielsen's Severity Scale (0 = cosmetic, 4 = usability catastrophe).
- Capture screenshots for each and write a 1-paragraph redesign recommendation.

### 3. Public Social Deliverable
- **X Thread / LinkedIn Post**: *"Why do developer tools feel so hostile? A software engineer's heuristic evaluation of [Tool] and 3 design flaws breaking user mental models."*

---

## Sprint 2 (Weeks 3–4): Needfinding & Qualitative Research

### 1. Key Readings
- Stanford CS147 Lecture Notes: *Needfinding and Qualitative Interviews* (James Landay).
- Virginia Braun & Victoria Clarke, *Using thematic analysis in psychology* (The standard framework for qualitative coding in HCI).
- Paper: *Observing the User Experience: A Practitioner's Guide to User Research* (Goodman, Kuniavsky, Moed).

### 2. The Field Work (Your Practical Task)
- Choose a domain problem (e.g., *How developers inspect and trust code generated by AI coding assistants*).
- Formulate a 1-page semi-structured interview script (5 demographic/warm-up questions + 8 open-ended probing questions).
- Interview **3 software engineers or colleagues** (20–30 mins each).
- Transcribe key quotes and group them onto an affinity board (or Obsidian canvas) to identify **2 core recurring themes / unmet needs**.

### 3. Public Social Deliverable
- **Social Post**: *"I interviewed 3 software engineers about how they verify LLM-generated code. Here are 3 unexpected cognitive workarounds people use that current tools ignore."*

---

## Sprint 3 (Weeks 5–6): Direct Manipulation & Interactive Prototyping

### 1. Key Readings
- Edwin Hutchins, James Hollan, Donald Norman, *Direct Manipulation Interfaces* (Human-Computer Interaction, 1985).
- Ben Shneiderman, *The Eyes Have It: A Task by Data Type Taxonomy for Information Visualizations* (IEEE VL 1996).
- Bret Victor, *Inventing on Principle* & *Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction* (Essential viewing).

### 2. The Field Work (Your Practical Task)
- Build a lightweight interactive prototype or UI probe (using React, Svelte, Streamlit, or web canvas) addressing one need from Sprint 2.
- Rules for the prototype:
  - Eliminate modal dialogs and pure text prompt waiting.
  - Implement *continuous representation of objects of interest*.
  - Provide rapid, incremental, reversible operations with immediate visual feedback.

### 3. Public Social Deliverable
- **Social Post + 30s Demo GIF**: *"Instead of asking developers to prompt an LLM blindly, I built a prototype applying direct manipulation principles to code generation. Here is what immediate feedback changes."*

---

## Sprint 4 (Weeks 7–8): Empirical Evaluation (User Studies & Metrics)

### 1. Key Readings
- Jonathan Lazar et al., *Research Methods in Human-Computer Interaction* (Chapters on Usability Testing & Surveys).
- John Brooke, *SUS: A 'Quick and Dirty' Usability Scale* (1996).
- Hart & Staveland, *Development of NASA-TLX (Task Load Index)*.

### 2. The Field Work (Your Practical Task)
- Recruit **5 participants** to test your Sprint 3 prototype.
- Run a formal **Think-Aloud Protocol**: Ask participants to complete 2 benchmark tasks while speaking their thought process aloud.
- Record:
  - Task completion time
  - Error rate / moments of confusion
  - 10-item System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire (calculate the 0–100 score).
- Summarize findings into a structured research report.

### 3. Public Social Deliverable
- **Social Post**: *"I put my prototype in front of 5 users using a Think-Aloud protocol. 3 things completely broke, my SUS score was [X], and here is what engineering intuition got wrong about user behavior."*

---

## Sprint 5 (Weeks 9–10): Human-Centered AI (HCAI) & Mixed-Initiative Systems

### 1. Key Readings
- Saleema Amershi et al., *Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction* (ACM CHI 2019, Best Paper).
- Eric Horvitz, *Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces* (ACM CHI 1999).
- Joon Sung Park et al., *Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior* (ACM UIST 2023).

### 2. The Field Work (Your Practical Task)
- Select an emerging AI tool (e.g. Claude Artifacts, Cursor Composer, GitHub Copilot Workspace, v0.dev).
- Benchmark it systematically against the **18 Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction**:
  - Initially (G1: Make clear what the system can do; G2: Make clear how well it can do it).
  - During interaction (G3–G6: Contextual matching, social norms).
  - When wrong (G7–G11: Support efficient invocation, dismissal, correction).
  - Over time (G12–G18: Learn from behavior, notify of changes).
- Document where AI agencies collide with human intent.

### 3. Public Social Deliverable
- **Newsletter Article / Deep Dive**: *"Are today's AI agent interfaces truly human-centered? An empirical audit against CHI guidelines."*

---

## Sprint 6 (Weeks 11–12): The Research Proposal & PhD Pitch

### 1. Key Readings
- Philip Guo, *The Ph.D. Grind* (Free online memoir on the reality of HCI PhDs).
- Amy J. Ko, *A Guide to Getting a PhD in HCI* (Bits and Behavior).
- Selected recent CHI/UIST Best Paper Award winner in your area of interest.

### 2. The Field Work (Your Practical Task)
- Synthesize your past 10 weeks of field studies and prototypes into a **2-page HCI Research Proposal**:
  1. **Motivation & Problem Statement** (The human friction).
  2. **Research Questions** (Framed around human cognition, capability, or collaboration).
  3. **Proposed Artifact / System Contribution** (Your engineering strength).
  4. **Evaluation Methodology** (How you will prove it works with real humans).
- Identify **5 professors** across top HCI institutions (CMU, Stanford, UW, MIT, Berkeley, GaTech, UCSD) whose lab output aligns with this proposal.

### 3. Public Social Deliverable
- **Portfolio / Milestone Post**: Share your 12-week retrospective, links to your prototypes, open notes, and announcement of your PhD research direction!
