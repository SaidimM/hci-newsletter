# 📘 Syllabus — HCI Foundations (Self-Directed, Fall 2026)

> **Student:** Saidi (software engineer → HCI PhD applicant)
> **Duration:** 12 weeks · Mon Oct 5 – Sun Dec 27, 2026
> **Workload:** Mon–Fri 2 h/day + Sat–Sun 4 h/day ≈ **18 h/week**
> **Format:** Lectures + readings + weekly homework (field work) + semester project + public write-ups

---

## 1. Course goals

By the end of this course I can:
1. **Explain** the core theories of HCI (gulfs, mental models, heuristics, human abilities, direct manipulation, distributed cognition, human-AI interaction) in my own words.
2. **Do** the core methods: heuristic evaluation, interviews, thematic analysis, task analysis, prototyping, think-aloud testing, controlled experiments with statistics.
3. **Complete** one full small HCI study, from needfinding to evaluation, and write it up as a CHI-style paper.
4. **Show** this publicly through a portfolio of posts that supports my PhD applications.

## 5. Assessment (self-graded, honestly)

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Weekly homework (11 × rubric) | 55% |
| Midterm | 10% |
| Final report | 25% |
| Public posts (≥ 1 per week) | 10% |

- Every homework has a rubric in its week file. Grade yourself in `grade.md`.
- **Below B → redo the weakest criterion** before marking the homework `graded`.
- **TA review:** paste your submission to the AI assistant and ask it to grade strictly against the rubric.

## 6. Policies

- **Due date:** every Sunday 23:59.
- **Late work:** Week 7 is the buffer. If you're more than 1 week behind, shift the whole schedule by a week. **Never skip homework** — homework is the course.
- **Research ethics:** always get verbal informed consent; refer to participants only as P1, P2…; never store recordings or identifiable data in Git; never publish identifiable quotes.
- **Participant numbering** continues across the semester (P1, P2, … P20+) so every person is uniquely traceable in your notes.
- **Own words rule:** concept notes are written in your own words, never copy-pasted.
